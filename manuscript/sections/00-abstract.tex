\begin{abstract}
A mobile client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact still holds after a lifecycle gap. Notification silence is not evidence: a background process receives callbacks late, a frozen one after thaw, and a killed or unregistered one never. We measure what Android offers instead for seven sources and four deployments, on Android 13 and 15 emulators and on rented Pixel~8 and Galaxy~S25 phones. First, provider stamps are candidates, not certificates. When a write landed between a value read and the following version read, the pair certified the old value after every update or insert to Contacts and MediaStore (\PhysRaceStampedStale on the Pixel~8 and \PhysSamsungRaceStampedStale on the S25), and a Contacts provider that was wiped and refilled kept its old stamp in \PhysResetSameStamp Pixel~8 trials. Second, the gap depends on the device. The cached-app freezer was off by default on \PhysFreezerOffTrialUnits Pixel~8 units, the S25 froze a cached client in \PhysSamsungFrozen attempts within \PhysSamsungFreezeWindowS\,s, and Samsung's Contacts provider marks its notifications with a hidden no-delay flag, so a background client heard Contacts changes after \PhysSamsungBgMedian but MediaStore changes only after \PhysSamsungMediaBgMedian. Third, re-validating just before an action leaves a check-to-effect window (median \PhysCteMs\,ms on the Pixel~8), and writes injected into that window still reached the effect. None of the stock sources we tested has an established snapshot and reset contract, so a sound client returns UNKNOWN and refreshes; for cheap queries refresh was also faster than optimistic stamp checks (\PhysCostGl versus \PhysCostLm\,ms per decision on the Pixel~8), and batched stamp checks through a host helper were faster only because they re-read fewer facts.
\end{abstract}
