\begin{abstract}
A client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact survived a lifecycle gap. Silence is not evidence: callbacks reach a background process late, a frozen one after thaw, and a killed one never. We measure what Android offers for seven sources and four deployments, on Android 13 and 15 emulators and on four rented models: Pixel~8 and Galaxy~S25 on Android 15, Pixel~9 on Android 16, which behaved like the Pixel~8, and Pixel~8a on Android 17. First, provider stamps are candidates, not certificates: a write between a value read and the next version read made the pair certify the old value after every Contacts or MediaStore update or insert (\PhysRaceStampedStale on each model that ran it), and a wiped and refilled Contacts provider kept its stamp in \PhysResetSameStamp Pixel~8 trials. Second, the gap depends on the device. The cached-app freezer was off on \PhysFreezerOffTrialUnits rented Pixel~8 units as provisioned; on the S25 Android's freezer never froze a cached client (\PhysSamsungLongAndroidFrozen samples), though in one run Samsung moved a long-cached client into its own frozen cgroup within \PhysSamsungLongToGroupS\,s of screen-off, whose effect on callbacks we did not test; and Samsung's Contacts provider sets a hidden no-delay flag, so background clients heard Contacts changes after \PhysSamsungBgMedian but MediaStore changes after \PhysSamsungMediaBgMedian. Third, re-validating just before an action leaves a check-to-effect window (median \PhysCteMsInt\,ms on the Pixel~8) that overlapping writes still reached. No stock source we tested has an established snapshot and reset contract, so a sound client returns UNKNOWN and refreshes, faster than optimistic stamp checks for cheap queries.
\end{abstract}
