\begin{abstract}
A mobile client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact still holds after a lifecycle gap. Notification silence is not evidence: a background process receives callbacks late, a frozen one after thaw, and a killed or unregistered one never. We measure what Android offers instead for seven sources and four deployments, on Android 13 and 15 emulators and on rented Pixel~8 and Galaxy~S25 phones. Three findings stand out. First, provider stamps are candidates, not certificates: reading a value and then its version certified a changed value in \PhysRaceStale of \PhysRaceTrials races on the Pixel~8 and \PhysSamsungRaceStale of \PhysSamsungRaceTrials on the S25, and a Contacts reset left the stamp unchanged in \PhysResetSameStamp Pixel~8 trials. Second, the gap depends on the device and its vendor's provider: Pixel~8 units differed in build and freezer default, the S25 froze a cached client in \PhysSamsungFrozen attempts, and its Contacts notifications carry a hidden no-delay flag, so a background client heard Contacts changes after \PhysSamsungBgMedian but MediaStore changes only after \PhysSamsungMediaBgMedian. Third, re-validating just before an action does not close the race: \PhysBndOverlapStale overlapping changes still produced stale effects. A conservative client therefore returns UNKNOWN without a complete snapshot and reset contract. In the client, direct refresh beats optimistic stamp validation for cheap provider queries; a host helper's batched stamp checks were faster only because they re-read fewer facts, under the same policy that failed the race. Input memoization saves model calls for derived facts.
\end{abstract}
