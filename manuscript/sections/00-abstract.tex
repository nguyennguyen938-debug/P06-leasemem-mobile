\begin{abstract}
A mobile client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact still holds after a lifecycle gap. Notification silence is not evidence: a background process receives callbacks late, a frozen one after thaw, and a killed or unregistered one never. We measure what Android offers instead for seven sources and four deployments, on Android 13 and 15 emulators and on three rented phone models: Pixel~8 and Galaxy~S25 on Android 15 and Pixel~9 on Android 16. First, provider stamps are candidates, not certificates. When a write landed between a value read and the following version read, the pair certified the old value after every update or insert to Contacts and MediaStore (\PhysRaceStampedStale, \PhysSamsungRaceStampedStale and \PhysNineRaceStampedStale on the three models), and a Contacts provider that was wiped and refilled kept its old stamp in \PhysResetSameStamp Pixel~8 trials. Second, the gap depends on the device. The cached-app freezer was off by default on \PhysFreezerOffTrialUnits Pixel~8 units; on the S25 the Android freezer froze a cached client in \PhysSamsungLongAndroidFrozen samples, but on one unit, after minutes cached, Samsung moved it into its own frozen cgroup within \PhysSamsungLongToGroupS\,s of screen-off; and Samsung's Contacts provider sets a hidden no-delay flag, so a background client heard Contacts changes after \PhysSamsungBgMedian but MediaStore changes only after \PhysSamsungMediaBgMedian. The Pixel~9 behaved like the Pixel~8. Third, re-validating just before an action leaves a check-to-effect window (median \PhysCteMs\,ms on the Pixel~8), and writes that overlapped the check still reached the effect. None of the stock sources we tested has an established snapshot and reset contract, so a sound client returns UNKNOWN and refreshes; refresh was also faster than optimistic stamp checks for cheap queries, and batched stamp checks through a host helper were faster only because they re-read fewer facts.
\end{abstract}
