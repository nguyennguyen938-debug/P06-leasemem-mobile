\begin{abstract}
A mobile client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact still holds after a lifecycle gap. The right strategy depends on what the gap does to notifications: a background process receives callbacks late, a frozen process after thaw, and a killed, force-stopped or unregistered process never, so notification silence is insufficient evidence. This measurement study maps these behaviors for seven Android sources and four deployments on Android 13 and 15 emulators and repeats the core experiments on rented \PhysDevice phones, whose units differ even in build and freezer setting. There, background callbacks took a median of \PhysDeployBgMedian, \PhysDeployFrozenBeforeThaw frozen trials received one before thaw, and after coverage-ending gaps event-only invalidation missed \PhysGapLostEv changes and restart-aware refresh \PhysGapLostEvr. Provider generations and row versions detect many staged changes, but a value read followed by a version read can certify the wrong value; timestamps and database resets introduce further gaps. Our evidence map therefore separates candidate stamps from freshness certificates: a conservative client returns UNKNOWN without a complete snapshot and reset contract. In the client, direct refresh is faster than optimistic stamp validation for cheap provider queries, but on the phone a host helper's batched stamp checks beat its batched refresh; input memoization saves model calls for derived facts. Arbitrary stock-provider certification remains open.
\end{abstract}
