\begin{abstract}
A mobile client that remembers phone state must decide whether a cached fact still holds after a lifecycle gap. The right strategy depends on what the gap does to notifications: a background process receives callbacks late, a frozen process after thaw, and a killed, force-stopped or unregistered process never, so notification silence is insufficient evidence. This measurement study maps these behaviors for seven Android sources and four deployments on Android 13 and 15 emulators and repeats the core experiments on rented \PhysDevice phones. There, background callbacks took a median of \PhysDeployBgMedian, \PhysDeployFrozenBeforeThaw frozen trials received one before thaw, and after coverage-ending gaps event-only invalidation missed \PhysGapLostEv changed uses and restart-aware refresh \PhysGapLostEvr. Provider generations and row versions detect many staged changes, but a value read followed by a version read can certify the wrong value; timestamps and database resets introduce further gaps. Our evidence map therefore separates candidate stamps from freshness certificates: a conservative client returns UNKNOWN without a complete snapshot and reset contract. For cheap provider queries, direct refresh is faster than optimistic stamp validation, and input memoization saves model calls for derived facts. Even units of one phone model differ (the cached-app freezer was off on \PhysFreezerOffUnits), so a client should not assume its deployment's lifecycle behavior. Arbitrary stock-provider certification remains open.
\end{abstract}
