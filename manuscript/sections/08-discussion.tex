\paragraph{Observe the lifecycle.} Our phone runs turn the cached-app freezer on (\S\ref{sec:phys-setup}) because it was off as provisioned on \PhysFreezerOffTrialUnits Pixel~8 and \PhysSamsungFreezerOffTrialUnits Galaxy~S25 units behind the reported trials. On the S25, Android's freezer never froze a cached client (\PhysSamsungLongAndroidFrozen samples). In one run, a client cached for \PhysSamsungLongScreenOnS\,s entered Samsung's own cgroup v1 frozen group within \PhysSamsungLongToGroupS\,s of screen-off. Freshly started clients on another unit entered it later or not at all (\PhysSamsungScreenLater, \PhysSamsungScreenNever never) and received both callbacks during screen-off (\PhysSamsungScreenCallbacks). Whether that group holds callbacks is therefore untested (Table~\ref{tab:phys-s25}). The deferred class differs between the models too. Samsung's Contacts provider flags writes through it as no-delay (\PhysSamsungFlagWrites), while an app's own \texttt{notifyChange} on the same URI keeps its flags (\PhysSamsungFlagNotify flagged) and waits \PhysSamsungFlagNotifyDelay, on one unit. A client therefore must not assume its lifecycle or callback delay; Step~1 of Table~\ref{tab:guidance} checks them at run time, and without that check every gap may lose coverage.

\paragraph{Foreground services on current Android.} An FGS keeps callbacks prompt, but apps targeting Android~14 must declare its type and permission (a memory client would declare \texttt{dataSync} or the Play-reviewed \texttt{specialUse})~\cite{android_fgstypes}, and for apps targeting Android~15 a \texttt{dataSync} service stops being foreground after six background hours in 24, until the user returns~\cite{android_fgstimeout}. Treat FGS residency as a window: an assistant can keep it to user-initiated tasks, well under that limit, and must refresh before the next critical use once the window ends.

\paragraph{External validity.} Physical results come from four rented models on Android~15 to 17, besides emulators on 13 and 15, so vendor, model and release cannot be separated, and the three newer models each ran a subset, mostly on one unit (Appendix~\ref{app:phys-setup}). On the Pixel~8a, refresh again beat \stamp and a verified long Doze only deferred callbacks (Table~\ref{tab:phys-eighta}). Under the same seeded schedule, the S25 and Pixel~9 matched the Pixel~8 trial for trial on all five sources (\PhysSamsungRaceSameAsPixel, \PhysNineRaceSameAsPixel), and on the S25 lost-coverage gaps again defeated event-only (\PhysSamsungGapLostEv missed, restart-aware \PhysSamsungGapLostEvr), but Contacts delivery and freezing differed, so other vendors and releases may differ again. The rented phones stay on the charger and their Doze is forced, so we have measured neither how often gaps occur in real use nor battery drain. A privacy-preserving beta could measure field frequency by logging only gap classes, callback lateness and verdict counts per source, never values.

\paragraph{Platform opportunities.} Closing the check-to-effect window needs a provider or OS change. Contacts writes already support update-if-version~\cite{android_rawcontacts,android_cpo}, but no call, message or share API takes such a condition; the minimal addition is effect-if-version, which the system rejects unless the row still has the certified version when the effect commits (Appendix~\ref{app:stock}). The appendix also lists what joined reads need and compares iOS change history.
